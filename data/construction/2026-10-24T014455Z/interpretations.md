## INT-007: protected payload and control traffic

Groups RG-007, RG-008, RG-029, RG-030, RG-035. Pages 10–13, sections 4 and 4.1.1–4.1.4 were read in original-page layout extraction. Page 10 says each classified packet/frame is encrypted first by Inner and then by Outer; page 12 distinguishes data, management, and control planes. Page 13 says there is no general requirement to separate necessary control traffic similarly.

Selected reading: finite execution claims track a protected payload introduced on a declared Red ingress. Control packets are a separate traffic class. Permitted IKE/ESP control does not constitute permission for an arbitrary classified payload to bypass a layer. Local applications cannot be silently treated as harmless control sources; local input/output hooks and initial origin conditions remain modeled or explicit limitations. Full security level includes all dimensions listed on page 10. Snapshot claims require justified source-domain attribution and complete applicable packet-processing state.

Competing reading rejected: classify every IP packet, including network control, as protected Red data. That reading contradicts the explicit traffic-plane definitions. General packet operations belong in Semantics/Execution; CSfC layer roles belong in Targets/Architecture and Targets/Boundary.

## INT-012: authentication method and full security level

Groups RG-008 and RG-012. Pages 15–18, sections 4.2.2–4.2.2.2 were read with surrounding same-level discussion and figure captions. Page 16 states “separate CAs, CAKs, and management devices are needed” at each security level, and explains that different levels trust CAs with no trust relationship or use different CAKs. The same page describes two distinct Secret U.S.-only networks at the same level and permits optional extra filtering.

Selected reading: separate the credential material applicable to the selected certificate or shared-key authentication method. Certificate-based peers need separate authority trust closures, while PSK peers need distinct CAK identities. Both branches need separate management devices across different full levels. Same-level network identity alone does not impose the cross-level prohibition. Figure 5 illustrates optional stronger separation; it does not change the full-level equality criterion.

Alternatives considered: require unused CAKs even for certificate-only implementations, or equate every different Red network with a different level. Those readings conflict with authentication alternatives and the explicit same-level example. Targets/AuthenticationIsolation defines credential-material separation and authentication exclusion separately. Authority identifiers must bind to actual verified key identities and effective trust closure; CN display strings alone cannot discharge that premise. A management-free data snapshot does not prove management-device presence or separation.

## INT-014: same-level filtering sentence

Groups RG-008, RG-014, RG-039, RG-040. Original pages 17–18, 23, 46–47 were checked. Page 23 says Outer filtering prevents Inner components “for networks of the same security level” from sending to each other. Page 17 requires intended same-level connectivity and forbids cross-level communication. MSC-PF-17 on page 46 permits source/destination Inner interfaces supporting the same level; PF-23 likewise allows appropriate same-level traffic.

Selected reading: the page-23 sentence refers to unintended same-level peer traffic, not a blanket prohibition on all same-level communication. Outer rules admit only intended peers at the same full level; distinguishability of address assignments supports that rule. An alternative reading treats “same” as an editorial error for “different”; both yield cross-level exclusion, but the selected reading additionally preserves intended-peer restrictions stated on page 17 without asserting an official textual correction. A blanket same-level ban conflicts with explicit PF-17/PF-23 and the purpose of the solution.

Formal consequence: address ownership is unique by full level, and firewall allow entries bind source and destination ranges to intended peer pairs. A same-level equality test alone is insufficient.

## INT-031: routing responsibility

Group RG-031. Original pages 13 and 23 and table requirements SR-17/OR-8 were read. Page 13 forbids routing protocols on Outer internal/external interfaces and says the Outer may not perform routing functionality. Selected reading: Outer devices do not provide an independent routing service or run dynamic routing protocols.
