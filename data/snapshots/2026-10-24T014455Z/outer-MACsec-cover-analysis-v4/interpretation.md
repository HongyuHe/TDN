# RG-032: explicit transmission-characteristic protection

The selected local interpretation requires an actual mechanism that changes observable transmission characteristics. An enabled Boolean or ordinary payload encryption does not establish that mechanism.

The original CP 1.3.0 PDF is 4e40e3d83708f33a033808d8362f78127973f53239dc03b72163fa6cfa5925c4. Direct readings of pages 23, 44 and 61 are retained in references/transec. Page 23 requires additional TRANSEC when the Black network is the Public Internet. Page 44 MSC-OR-9 places an objective requirement on Outer Encryption with an external MACsec interface connected to the Outer Firewall. Page 61 defines controls against interception, disrupted reception, communications deception and/or intelligence derived from transmission characteristics. The different force and conditions remain explicit. The local fixture selects the MACsec-to-Outer-Firewall case; it does not assert that its local bridge is the real Public Internet.

The chosen mechanism masks the size and approximate rate of selected protected data during an active laboratory window. A custom shim runs before each actual Outer MACsec controlled port. It sends one fixed 512-byte record in each configured 20 ms slot. An empty queue produces an indistinguishable padded record inside MACsec. A nonempty queue contributes one IP packet of up to 488 bytes. The peer discards padding or injects the recovered packet into a TUN interface. Inner IPsec remains applied before the shim. The bridge Outer Firewall forwards MACsec frames only.

The source interpretation treats size/rate masking as one concrete control against analysis of message externals. RFC 9347 explains that fixed packet sizes and a constant send rate can hide size/frequency, while RFC 4303 describes padding and dummy packets as limited traffic-flow confidentiality. The official IEEE 802.1AEdk project description independently discusses MAC privacy through aggregation, padding and controlled frame sizes/timing. Those resources explain the selected mechanism. They do not make this custom wire format conform to either standard or establish its sufficiency for an approving authority.

The implemented rule is a FIFO with 64 packet capacity. New packets are dropped when the queue is full or when a packet exceeds 488 bytes. Late software scheduling skips missed slots and restarts from the current monotonic time. Link failure produces recorded send errors. The final experiment handles AF_PACKET ENETDOWN without discarding the TUN interface. The initial failure remains preserved. Software/kernel clocks, scheduling, live links, trusted provisioning and adequate capacity are explicit assumptions.

The observations bind useful bidirectional payload recovery, Inner and Outer loss/restoration, exact settings, full forwarding state, public wire headers and measured idle/load/idle intervals. The observed 558-byte MACsec frames are uniform in those windows. The observations do not prove a hard real-time schedule, long-term indistinguishability, arbitrary-load behavior, protection against jamming or physical interception, approved hardware, or full TRANSEC compliance. SCI, packet numbers, endpoint timing, failures and clock jitter remain visible. Construction must connect the exact implemented emission/queue model to its stated conditional guarantee rather than promote configured timing into an unconditional OS guarantee.

The decision date is 2026-10-24. The Evidence Agent selected the mechanism after original-source review and coordinator authorization. Whole RG-032 remains open while substantive formal guarantees, deployment scope and applicable external assurance remain unsettled.
